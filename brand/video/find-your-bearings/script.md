# Find Your Bearings — video script (EN)

- **Status:** v1, cloud session, 2026-11-19. Transcreation of the original Hungarian game (YouTube `UW6tIK_JSKE`, 2016), not a translation. Sophie approved the plan; this text is for her read.
- **Voice:** Sophie's vidIQ clone ("Sophie – Saját hang"). Sophie may re-record later.
- **Fixed, never change:** the seven symbols and their order (cube, ladder, lion, road, sea, house + sign, horse), the lion dissolving, the questions themselves. The key (on the page below the video) depends on them.
- **Copy rules:** singular "they/their", never "his/her" (Sophie, 2026-11-19). The script never names what a symbol means and never describes it beyond the original's questions: the viewer's own image is the whole point.
- **Format:** each `>` line is one voice line. `[gap N]` = seconds of silence after it (breath between lines). `[WRITE N]` = a writing pause with the "Pause and write it down" card and music only.

## 0 · Title (music only, ~5 s)

On screen: *Find Your Bearings* · RECHART logo.

## 1 · Taking your bearings

> Before a navigator sets off anywhere, they do one small thing first. They take their bearings: where am I standing, right now? [gap 1.2]

> That's what we're going to do together. Only, this time, the map is inside you. [gap 1.5]

> Keep a pen and paper close. You can pause the video at any point to write your answers down, and you can always come back to it later. [gap 1.2]

> Turn your inner imagination right up, be honest, and always go with your very first impression. [gap 1]

> Don't worry, there are no wrong answers here. Only symbols hiding deep in your mind, and at the end, I'll help you read them. [gap 1.2]

> Ready? Let's go. [gap 2.5]

## 2 · The cube

> Imagine you're standing in the middle of a vast, beautiful desert. [gap 1.5]

> Your eyes rest on the soft curves of the dunes. Near and far, there's nothing at all… [gap 1]

> …except a cube, which catches your eye. [gap 1.5]

> Here's your first task. Tell me, what is this cube like? [gap 1]

> What colour is it? How big? What is it made of? Where exactly is it? [gap 1]

> What are its edges like? And its surface? Have a really good look. [WRITE 14]

## 3 · The ladder

> Not long after, you discover a ladder. [gap 1.2]

> What is this ladder like? What's it made of? Does it have one side, or two? [gap 1]

> Does it look old, or new? Does it stand up on its own, or does it have to lean against something? [gap 1]

> Would you dare to step onto it? [gap 1.2]

> If, in the pictures in your head, you reach out and touch these things, that's fine too. Let your imaginary self move around freely. [WRITE 12]

## 4 · The lion

> Then, all of a sudden, you hear a growl from behind the nearest dune. [gap 1]

> A lion appears, and it's quite clear it's about to attack. [gap 1]

> Quick, what do you do? [WRITE 10]

> Written it down? Good. Then here's the good news. [gap 1]

> Maybe it's the heat, maybe something else, but before anything can happen, the lion simply dissolves into thin air. You must have imagined it. [gap 2]

## 5 · The road

> Carry on, because soon you'll come across a road. [gap 1]

> Use your inner eyes again. What is this road like? Take a good look. [gap 1]

> Is it straight, or does it wind? Does it look as if people walk it often? What might it be for? [gap 1]

> Is it paved? Notice everything. [WRITE 12]

## 6 · The sea

> Set off along it. And once you have, before long you'll hear gulls crying in the distance… [gap 1]

> …and it doesn't take much more before you come out at the sea. [gap 1.5]

> Take a deep breath, and look around. Write down what you see. [gap 1]

> What's the weather like? What's the water like: calm and quiet, or rough? Clear, or murky? [gap 1]

> And the shore: is it sand, pebbles, or rock? [gap 1]

> Let yourself notice everything. [WRITE 14]

## 7 · The house and the sign

> In the distance, you spot a house. You set off towards it… [gap 1]

> …but then you notice a sign, too. Hmm. Is there anything written on it? [gap 1.2]

> If there isn't, let it be. Just keep walking. [gap 1.5]

> Start looking at the house, because you can make out more and more of it. [gap 1]

> Tell me, what is it like? What is it built from? How does it make you feel? [gap 1]

> Does anyone live there? Gather up everything that comes to you. [WRITE 14]

## 8 · The horse

> And finally, just before you reach the house, a horse appears. [gap 1.2]

> What is it like? What does it do when it sees you? [gap 1]

> And what does it do after that? Write everything down. [WRITE 12]

## 9 · Landfall

> And that's it, that was the whole game. How was it? [gap 1.5]

> Look at what's on your page. That's where you're standing right now: your bearings. [gap 1.5]

> And from here, a whole journey opens up, if you'd like to take it. [gap 1.5]

> Your key is waiting for you just below this video. Come and see what your symbols are telling you. [gap 2]

## 10 · End card (music only, ~5 s)

## Visual rules (for whoever builds the video)

- **Never show a symbol** (no cube, ladder, lion, road, sea, house, sign or horse), and nothing that answers a question (no weather, no water, no colours of a cube). Showing one hands the viewer our image instead of theirs and breaks the test.
- Stills only, slow Ken Burns at most. Palette and type from the brand: paper `#FBF1E3`, ink `#142230`, deep sea `#1C4F6B`, copper `#B87345`/`#8A5129`; Cormorant Garamond, Lora, Shadows Into Light Two.
- Title and intro: the sextant watercolor (`site/public/images/wayfinder/sextant.webp`). Sections 2–5: one empty watercolor desert (dunes, sky, nothing else). Sections 6–8: near-empty paper, only a faint horizon line, no colour of water or weather. Section 9: the sunset sailboat (`hero-sailboat-sunset-banner.webp`), since by then every answer is written.
- A small route map (the RECHART dashed copper line) with seven dots, one lighting up per station, gives progress without giving content. Dots are numbered, never labelled with the symbol.
- WRITE pauses: paper card, "Pause and write it down" in the hand font, a pen line drawing, the station number.
