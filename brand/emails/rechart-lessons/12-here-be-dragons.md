Hi {{Nickname}},

Your map is finished. There's a big X on it. You know where you're going.

And I'd bet that somewhere in the last two days, a small voice has piped up. *Who do you think you are? That's not for people like you. What if it goes wrong?*

Of course it has. That's not a sign that something's wrong with you, or with your X. It's a sign the X is real. Nobody gets nervous about a goal they don't care about.

Old mapmakers had a lovely habit. Where the known world ended, at the edges, in the unexplored waters, they drew sea monsters. On one old globe, from around 1510, there's even a little Latin label: *hic sunt dracones.* Here be dragons.

So today, that's where we're going. To the edges of your map. To meet the dragons.

## The company you keep

You'll know the most popular picture of the fact that we're made of different parts: the little angel and the little devil sitting on your shoulders. The angel always nudges you towards good deeds, the devil towards spite, envy and power over others.

Well. The reality is a good deal more complicated.

Because you really are made of parts, and sometimes they seem to be completely at odds with each other. There's the part of you that wants to rest, and the part that would never stop working, so you'll be financially secure.

There's the part that would travel the whole world, and the part that sounds the alarm about everything that could go wrong.

There's the part that says go and tell the boss about the colleague who did no work at all, so they don't take the credit, and the part that's scared of the conflict.

A cart pulled in two directions doesn't usually get very far. And we'd like to get somewhere.

The first and most important thing about these parts, and I'd treat it as a working hypothesis, because it has always worked for me:

**assume that every single one of them wants what's GOOD for you.**

It wants you to be successful and happy. It just has its own rather particular logic about how you should get there. Some of them would hold you back from certain things like mad, because they're afraid you'll get hurt. What if it doesn't work out?

The favourite weapons of our contradicting parts are doubts and fears. Little trolls, if you like. They're the ones who whisper: why do you want a new job? You'll only mess it up. Why go and talk to that person you like? They'll only turn you down and laugh. Why stand up in front of people and say what you want to say? They'll only make fun of you. You can't do it. You're not enough. You don't deserve it.

## Who sits in the front

There's a writer I'm very fond of, Elizabeth Gilbert, who for me has painted the kindest picture of all this. She tells it in interviews, and in her book *Big Magic*. Let me retell it in my own words.

She imagines every new creative adventure as a long road trip. And all of her is in the car: her creativity, her ego, her fear, her uncertainty, the lot. They're all part of her, so none of them can be thrown out. They're all coming.

But there's a rule. She and her creativity sit in the front. Creativity holds the map, she drives, and the two of them chat away about where to go and what to do. The others are allowed to come along, and they're allowed to talk. But they sit in the back. And when they start squabbling and shrieking like kids on a long journey, she turns round and says, roughly: *shh, Mummy's driving.*

What fear is never allowed to do is take the wheel. Because she knows the only decision fear ever makes is this one: stop the car.

## Fear at the wheel

When I was about ten or twelve, I went roller skating with my friends around our neighbourhood. There was an underpass you could get down into by the stairs, but for strollers and bikes there was a spiral ramp, and we decided we'd roll down that. I went last. At first there was no problem at all, the slope was fairly gentle... but as we went on, things sped up, and braking became impossible.

Within seconds, panic took over. Fear simply climbed into my head, looked out through my eyes, saw that all was lost, and decided we HAD TO STOP, AT ANY COST, because we couldn't control what was happening! We must! And Fear simply pushed me into the wall while I was rolling at an incredible speed. Naturally. If I fall over, I'll slow down.

It was a spectacular wipeout. Grazed knees and elbows. And as I sat there on the ground, Fear was nowhere to be found. Only Shame and Pain were left, while my friends, in their own lovely way, used the speed to get almost to the end of the underpass, braked calmly, and turned round to look at me.

That's my first memory of Fear simply taking the wheel from me and making a terrible decision, one that came with a lot of pain and a lot of shame.

Slowly, I understood that we can't run away from our fears. They'll always be there. If we push them down, they jump out at the worst possible moment. The deeper we bury them, the harder they burst out.

So what can we do?

We can always recognise a fear. It has a nice, clear voice. But the wheel is in our hands... so what then?

We give fear a seat. We speak to it, call it by its name, and acknowledge that it's there. We promise to listen to it. That it can always say what it wants to say, because we know that deep down it wants what's good for us. It's trying to protect us from something. But we never hand it the decision. It can be a passenger at most. It can talk, and talk, and talk. But we're the ones driving.

## Task 1 · The Doubt envelope

Any object you choose would do, really. But since your values already have an envelope, I say your doubts should have one too. That's where they'll live. We're not hiding from them.

Whenever you lose heart while planning your goals, don't turn that voice down. Turn it up, until it can say what it means clearly, in one sentence.

Then write that sentence on a card, and put it in the envelope. Write on the front: **Doubt envelope.**

## Task 2 · Questioning

Once a few have gathered, open the envelope and pick the fear that bothers you most. The one that other parts of you disagree with.

Ask the fear:

- What do you want to protect me from?
- What do you want to spare me?
- What do you want for me?

You see, every fear has a higher purpose too. Be brave, and ask it: *what are you trying to achieve by...?*

And then listen to what it has to say.

For example. The fear sounds like this: *"I'm scared to change jobs."*

What do you want to protect me from?
*Uncertainty.*
What do you want to spare me?
*Swapping a safe bad thing for an uncertain one.*
What do you want for me?
*That you're calm and safe.*
So what do you wish me?
*Peace and safety.*

The answers will be your own, of course. Sometimes they'll be vague, or make no sense at first. But if you keep asking, patiently, that rather particular logic I mentioned starts to unfold.

And you can honestly thank it. Yes, you'd like to be calm and safe too. But you'd also like to reach your goals, and find interesting, exciting work you actually enjoy.

## Task 3 · Brainstorming

Look for alternatives you can offer the fear. Use very simple logic.

Ask: *when would I feel calm and safe?*

When you have some savings put by, and a secure job.

So what could you offer this fear? Maybe that you put a little aside every month? That you won't hand in your notice until you have a new offer? That you start actively looking, so your decision is better grounded? That you start learning a new skill?

Our fears, if we ask them patiently and kindly, can give us an awful lot of extra ideas.

Write these answers on the back of the card.

## Task 4 · Re-measuring

Look at the card with the fear on it again. How do you feel about that sentence now?

Sometimes a fear you've worked with like this goes completely quiet. Sometimes one round isn't enough.

If it doesn't feel right yet, put it back in the Doubt envelope.

And if you've been able to let it go, well, a second envelope makes a lovely home for it. Write on that one: **Tamed Dragons.**

## Your map · The dragons

Take out your map.

For every card in your Doubt envelope, draw a small dragon in the margin, out in the far waters near the edge of the sheet. Give it a name if you like. Make it as fierce or as silly as it feels.

When one of them moves into the Tamed Dragons envelope, go back to the map and give its dragon something to show it: a little collar, a bow, a sleepy face, whatever makes you smile.

They stay on the map. That's the point. A good navigator doesn't pretend there are no dragons. They know exactly where they are, and they sail anyway.

I've seen so many motivational films that suggest we have to fight our fears. Defeat them, crush them, triumph over them.

I don't think so. I don't think you could do anything braver than what's in this letter.

And you know, {{Nickname}}, I think you're rather brave. By now that imaginary wheel is settling nicely into your hands, and the way ahead is getting clearer. Never give up the driver's seat. But always listen to the real, real message of your doubts.

Next time, we plan the voyage.

With love,
Sophie

**+1 homework · Repaint the dragon**

Remember the therapeutic selfie, back in lesson 2? There was a particular setting, colour and angle of light that, by your own way of perceiving, showed you in the best light. Those were the submodalities you connect with good feelings.

But what about the ones you connect with bad feelings?

NLP calls what we're about to do *reframing*, and I'd love you to try it.

Pick one of your fears or doubts, one that wouldn't quieten down even after all of the above.

Notice how it appears in your mind's eye. Do you see a picture, hear a sound? If so, where? Above your head, at stomach height, next to your shoulder? Is it dark, black and white? If it's a picture, is it moving like a film, or still? Identify as many of the sensory qualities of this bad impression as you can, as you think about it.

Then step out of it, and call up the good qualities you found with your therapeutic selfie.

Take the bad picture, and with your imagination, start moving the good qualities onto it. If it was dark, brighten it. If it comes with a shrill, high-pitched squeak, make the voice deeper. If it was close, move it further away. If it had no colour, colour it in!

And all the while, don't forget to notice how the feelings inside you change. Play with it until you get the best result you can.
