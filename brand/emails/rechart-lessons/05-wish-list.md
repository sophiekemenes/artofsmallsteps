**Subject:** Lesson 5: Got your pen? Today it gets to work

**Preview text:** Five minutes of free writing, a wish list with no limits, and a faulty genie.

Hi {{Nickname}},

Come and let's talk a little about what we've been doing lately.

Calling on several different methods, we've kept setting challenges for our brain and our nervous system.

That brain, which may have settled into certain habits and situations, has now been jolted out of its usual rut.

A lot has stirred in you along the way. We've taught our brain new things, you've started paying attention differently, reacting differently.

The words where we paused for a moment and swapped them for something else.

Setting the focus of our attention during the day on the joyful things.

We've knocked the engine off the rails of habit, and set off towards completely new landscapes.

We've started working differently, we've got in touch with the downstairs neighbour, the unconscious.

If you've done everything up to this point (or even just half of it),

NOW your mind is ready for recharting.

Now there's enough attention in you to listen both outwards and inwards, to have a proper conversation with your deeper goals, and to start making them happen.

Today's task is a bit longer and more complex.

{{Nickname}}, have you got your pen? :)

Because for the first time, we're going to use the toolkit of intuitive writing. There's enormous power in writing by hand (in all kinds of writing, really, but the trio of hand, pen and paper still beats everything).

Think about it: what other tool do you have for laying your thoughts out in front of the world? As a child, speech simply "sticks" to you. But to learn to write, you made a really serious effort as a child. You could say it was the very first challenge the world set you in your life. The feelings and thoughts inside you become understandable to others in the form of shapes and symbols, that is, letters.

But at the same time, writing was also one of the very first things through which you, as a child, met expectations properly for the first time, and the possibility of making mistakes, the idea of failure. It was the first time you met the framework that regulated you: that loop had better be chubby enough, that z wavy enough, that o round enough, or else.

It was the first time they made you believe you were better or worse than someone, when you got your marks for it. The first time you were ranked somewhere, compared to the others.

So we can say that writing has already shaped the development of your identity and your personality in all sorts of ways, but it's now, as an adult, that you can really unfold the possibilities hidden in it.

Writing is one of the first creative, making processes you can find where the conscious and the unconscious join forces. Do you know how much moves in you, from the fine motor movements of your fingers to the sped-up conversation between the downstairs and the upstairs neighbour?

I love this so, so much.

Let yourself go with it!

Here's what we start with:

## 1 · Free writing

I'd like you to sit down in front of your notebook, set your clock to a five-minute countdown, and start writing. And listen, because sometimes this is the hardest part: it doesn't matter what.

I just want you to write. If nothing comes to mind, write "nothing comes to mind". Then something will. Write freely, without barriers, letter after letter. You can put doubts into words, or questions, or muse over the ingredients of a shepherd's pie.

It doesn't need to be legible, nobody else will read it. Maybe not even you. You don't need to bother with punctuation either. You don't need to pay attention to anything at all, really. For five minutes let there be nothing but you, and the scratch of the pen on the paper.

You don't have to show this "scribble" to anyone, and we won't do anything with it either.

But once the five minutes are up, please spend another minute or two checking in on how you feel. What was it like to sink into this light trance? What came out of you first? Complaints? Neutral things? What's the tone of your scribble as a whole? Do you like it, do you recognise yourself in it?

## 2 · Wish list

Give this exercise five minutes too.

Please start writing a list.

A list of all your wishes and longings, everything you'd like to come true in your life.

Everything counts. We're not looking at what's achievable now, there are no criteria, only the secret wishes hiding in your heart. Be brave and be shamelessly confident: write as if you were entitled to them, whatever they are!

As if the postman had delivered a faulty genie, one who lets you wish for a million things instead of three! Get carried away, and keep writing the list for as long as feels right.

## 3 · One step back

When the list is ready, do something else for a while.

Leave it for a couple of hours, watch a film, make dinner, jolt yourself out of it.

Then come back to the list and look over your wishes. Which one pulls you the most? Which one shines out from the rest? Which one would you want to reach first? And second?

Choose three.

The three that matter most to you.

## 4 · The closing chord

I'd like you to gather which inner qualities, which human values would matter most for these to come true.

For now, don't worry about outside resources, which one would need money, other people's help, or anything else from the outside world. Write down which inner states of mind, all together, someone would need to reach these goals.

If your goal is to get fitter, for example, the qualities you're looking for might be willpower, enthusiasm, persistence. If you long for a new job, it might be courage, self-confidence, faith.

These are only examples. Look for what inner values, according to your own inner map, the person who can reach these goals has.

Find as many as you can, but at least ten, write them down and keep them within reach. You'll need them next time we talk. :)

All the best!
