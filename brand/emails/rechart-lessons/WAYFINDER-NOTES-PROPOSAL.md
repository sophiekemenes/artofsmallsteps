# "From the first mate's logbook": wayfinding notes for lessons 1–6 (APPROVED by Sophie 2026-11-19, built into lessons 1–6)

Sophie is the reader's first mate (the sales page says so: "you're the captain. I'm the first mate."), so the box is her logbook. One short, boxed note per lesson, always in the same place (right before the sign-off), always with the same small heading. It ties the sales page's line ("inspired by the way the Polynesian wayfinders sail") to the course, gently and accurately. It never claims the course teaches wayfinding, and it never makes the reader a navigator in the traditional sense.

**Accuracy rules for these notes:**
- Mau Piailug, who guided Hōkūleʻa in 1976, was from Satawal in **Micronesia**, not Polynesia. The notes say "Pacific" or name the place correctly.
- No song lyrics (Moana / Vaiana): Disney's lyrics are copyrighted, and RECHART is a paid product. Naming the film is fine; quoting the songs isn't.
- No invented quotes. Where a person is mentioned, the note paraphrases what's on record.

Sources: [Hōkūleʻa, The Pwo Ceremony](https://worldwidevoyage.hokulea.com/moolelo-pwo-ceremony/) · [Hōkūleʻa archive, 2007 voyage for Mau](https://archive.hokulea.com/holokai/2007/mau_6_pwo.html) · [JSTOR Daily on Mau Piailug](https://daily.jstor.org/pius-mau-piailug-master-navigator-of-micronesia) · [Adventure Journal: Mau Piailug followed the stars to Tahiti](https://www.adventure-journal.com/2019/03/mau-piailug-one-of-the-last-wayfinders-followed-the-stars-to-tahiti/)

### Lesson 1 · The Downstairs Neighbour

> **From the first mate's logbook**
> In 1976 a double-hulled canoe called Hōkūleʻa sailed from Hawaiʻi to Tahiti, about 2,500 miles, with no compass, no charts and no instruments at all. Its navigator, Mau Piailug from the tiny island of Satawal, read the stars, the wind, the birds and the swell. A lot of what he knew couldn't be written down. It was felt. That's the kind of navigator your downstairs neighbour is.
>
> *About this little box: on the original page I promised to be your first mate, and a good first mate keeps a logbook. There’s no female version, by the way. I checked. “First materess” sounds far too much like something people want to sleep on, and I need you awake and curious all along the journey :)*

### Lesson 2 · The Protagonist

> **From the first mate's logbook**
> Nainoa Thompson, the Hawaiian navigator who learned from Mau, has said that the hardest thing was learning to read the ocean swells the way his teacher could, because no two people sense things in quite the same way. Same ocean, different reading. Your filters aren't a flaw in the instrument. They are the instrument.

### Lesson 3 · Added Value

> **From the first mate's logbook**
> The wayfinders' star compass isn't an object. It's held in the navigator's mind: the horizon divided into 32 "houses", where the stars rise and set. Nobody can hand it to you, you build it inside. (Your pen is a different matter. That one, somebody just did hand you.)

### Lesson 4 · Three Magic Words

> **From the first mate's logbook**
> Some Pacific navigators describe the voyage in a way that sounds the wrong way round to us: the canoe stays still, and the islands move past it. It's the same journey, just told from a different seat. Changing the words you use about your life works a bit like that.

### Lesson 5 · Wish List

> **From the first mate's logbook**
> Out at sea, wayfinders watch the birds. Some seabirds fly out to fish in the morning and head back to land at dusk, so the direction they fly in the evening points towards an island you can't see yet. Your wishes can work like that. They don't have to be the land. They show you where it is.

### Lesson 6 · Finger Exercise (end of Stage 1)

> **From the first mate's logbook**
> In the Caroline Islands, becoming a master navigator is marked with a ceremony called *pwo*. In 2007, on Satawal, Mau Piailug held one for the first time there in about fifty years, and initiated five Hawaiians, Nainoa Thompson among them, alongside eleven Micronesians. That title belongs to its tradition and to a lifetime of training, and a course doesn't hand it out. But the thing it honours, a navigator who trusts what they feel and knows how to read it, is exactly what we've been waking up in you these past ten days.
