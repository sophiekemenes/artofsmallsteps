Hi {{Nickname}},

The days are going by, and you might be wondering when we'll actually start *doing* something for our goals. You might even ask me: Sophie, are we only ever going to plan? When do we finally do something?

And I might ask you back: {{Nickname}}, do you really think planning isn't doing? Come on.

Because it is. In fact, everything is doing. If you sit and stare into space, that's doing too: you're sitting, and staring into space. It's all a question of point of view.

Just as it's a point of view how well what you do agrees with your most important values. And how far what you do takes you towards your lighthouse.

Everything is a point of view, and that's a point of view too.

In general, though, the more points of view you know about something, the more accurate your picture of it gets. There's a famous line by Alfred Korzybski (NLP borrowed it later): *the map is not the territory.* Reality and what you believe or perceive to be reality are two different things. BUT the more sides you look at reality from, the richer your picture of it becomes. Which, given that we're drawing a map, is rather handy.

Right now we're not reshaping your picture of the world, but your picture of yourself. And for that I'd like to give you one more very useful point of view.

You have roles in life.

## Roles

I don't mean Romeo, Juliet or Uncle Vanya. If you're an actor, I'm not talking about your stage roles, but about the ones off stage.

A role is a bundle of values and abilities. (Oh, I put that so nicely!) (It's true, by the way.) And you have to hold your own in a great many roles.

For example, one role might be: parent. The main values of the parent role are the children, family, safety, trust, protection, and so on. Its main abilities are bringing up children, teaching, caring, comforting, and, if we come down to earth, picking up toys from the floor for the hundredth time.

I'm only listing examples, because being a parent is one of the most serious roles there is. Whole books have been written about it. I'm giving it a few lines. But you see what I mean.

Drinking buddy is a role too. The main values here are a decent wallet, a sense of humour, conversation skills, and how well you hold your drink. The last few are abilities too, along with plenty of others, because there are qualities involved here as well. Sadly, there are terrible parents and hopeless drinking buddies. But there are also wonderful parents worth their weight in gold, and brilliant, kind, clever drinking buddies.

## Roles and quality

This is important to understand. Two more points of view: there are roles, so you have roles too. And your picture of yourself becomes a positive one when you can live those roles at a high quality.

## Task 1 · All your roles

Write down all the roles you have in life.

Big ones and small ones. For example:

parent, partner, friend, colleague, consultant, angler, car mechanic, kitchen wizard, kitchen disaster, lover, tailor, adviser, interior designer, the shoulder everyone cries on, tour guide, the joker, mediator, gladiator, terminator (those last ones only symbolically, of course).

Or: citizen, bus passenger, driver, communicator, boss, employee, brother or sister, neighbour, somebody's somebody, something's something.

Think about your own life, or go through one of your days, and write down which roles you're in, or which ones you feel you should be in.

Write them all down. Take as long as you like, or a little longer, so there's time for a coffee too.

## Task 2 · Five role cards

Now write your five most important roles on cards, one per card. (Same size as your value cards. If you can't be bothered cutting, use sheets of paper and leave some space under each one.)

On the back of each card, or in that space, write the most important values for that role. Then list the abilities you need in it. Finally, colour the card in, as much as shows how satisfied you are with yourself in that role, how truly you play it. If you don't feel like shading, just write a percentage.

For example:

**Role:** friend
**Values:** attention, understanding, generosity, love, appreciation
**Abilities:** listening well, being there, communication, support, comfort
**Satisfaction:** 70%

**Role:** bank employee
**Values:** financial security, calm, predictability
**Abilities:** cheerful early rising, routine
**Satisfaction:** 5%

**Role:** partner
**Values:** closeness, trust, fun, tenderness, a little adventure
**Abilities:** listening, affection, humour, saying sorry first, remembering how they take their coffee
**Satisfaction:** 30%

Now you have five carefully thought-through roles. All of them matter.

Put them in order of importance.

## Is it yours, or is it a label?

Before we go on, one important turn around roles. A role you feel is yours can be yours in two different ways.

One: it's part of your identity. It comes from your own choices or longings.

The other: it belongs to a label.

A role that comes from your identity comes from deep inside you, and sometimes it blossoms regardless of circumstances. We all know people who never had children of their own, and yet have every mark of a wonderful parent. And people who've raised several children and never really live the role. Some people are marvellous adventurers without ever moving an inch. Some have a beautiful singing voice and never perform anywhere.

A label, on the other hand, comes from other people's definitions. Typical ones: "single", "the boss", "the leader", "the teacher's pet", "the troublemaker", "the weird one", "the one who'll never settle down", "the star". Labels are what other people, or society, think about us, based on our situation or what we've done.

Labels are powerful, and it's almost impossible to escape their influence completely. Say you take a liking to someone at a party, and you tell a friend, who waves a hand and says: "Oh, forget it, that one's a heartbreaker." Unless that happens to appeal to you, that verdict will usually nip the romance in the bud. In the same way, the label "forbidden fruit" always makes something more desirable.

And they can be tricky, because you can't always tell whether a role reached you from the inside out, or from the outside in. That matters only because the first kind instinctively agrees with your inner values far more than the second.

Once, when I was little, seven or eight, and not exactly swimming in pocket money, I stole a chocolate bar from a shop. (A Kapucíner, I think, a Hungarian brand.) I did it very badly, of course: I stood in front of the chocolate shelf for at least a minute, then grabbed one with a sudden movement and half ran out of the shop crying "Ow, ow, my tummy hurts!"

Are you surprised that the shopkeeper came after me, and I gave the chocolate back, very sheepishly? Nobody called me a thief, although it would have been the perfect moment to stick that label on me. And I wasn't a thief. I'd made a decision, a bad one, obviously, and in a moment, in the shopkeeper's eyes, I'd become something I wasn't, on the inside.

Fact: I did something, and I had to face the consequences. But whether I do it again is up to me. The people who hand out labels don't care about that. I do, very much.

And most labels don't land on us that suddenly. A long, long time goes by, and sometimes they settle right into our picture of ourselves. That something's off isn't always visible. But sometimes you notice it, by the fact that you're never quite at peace with that role.

A small interlude, so you don't think I'm against labels. We need them, or humanity would drown in chaos. We have to compare and measure, because we've chosen to live together not only as individuals but as bigger groups, as societies. And for that we need some shared reference points.

So I won't talk you out of it either, if one of your heart's desires is to earn a label. To be "somebody", say, or a star. Anything you go after with deep self-knowledge is important and useful for you.

But it's good to know the difference, isn't it?

## Task 3 · Role scan

Take your role cards, and look at each one closely. Which roles do you feel are part of your identity, and which ones less so? Look at the values that go with them too. When you look at them, do you feel satisfied? Do they really fulfil you, calm you, feel like *you*?

The ones you have doubts about: put them aside. Or, just the opposite, write new goals for exactly those.

## Your map · Layer 4: the islands

Back to your big sheet.

Between your home port and your lighthouse there's a lot of empty sea. Let's put some land in it.

Draw each of your five roles as an island, anywhere in the sea. Big or small, round or rocky, whatever feels right. Write the role's name on it.

- If the role feels truly yours, draw its coastline with a firm, confident line.
- If it feels more like a label someone stuck on you, draw the coastline dotted.

Then colour each island in, as far as you shaded its card. Some will be lush and green. Some will be barely more than an outline. Both tell you something.
