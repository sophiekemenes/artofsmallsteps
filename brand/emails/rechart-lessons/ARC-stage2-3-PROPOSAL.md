## The guiding thread: you draw your other map, by hand

From lesson 7 on, the reader draws one real map on one big sheet of paper (A3, or two A4s taped together), adding one layer per lesson with the coloured pens from the packing list. By the end of Stage 2 the map is finished; Stage 3 gets the vessel ready for the weather on the way there.

## Why this order

1. **Map first, then sail.** Every goal-finding exercise comes before the fears and the plan, which is what the RECHART metaphor promises.
2. **Fears move from the middle of mapping to Stage 3.** In the HU order the fear letter (L10) sits between the higher purpose and the roles. Fears are loudest once a concrete goal exists, so they land better right after the X is marked, next to the obstacles. This matches the RECHART spec's Stage 3 ("obstacles, resources, practical constraints… staying in motion when the weather changes").
3. **The role scan now comes before the goals.** In the HU order, goals are written from roles in L11, and only in L12 do we check which roles are really ours. Swapped, the goals are built on roles that have already been checked.
4. **Each lesson still has a playful task**, so it doesn't turn into a paperwork stretch: drawing the map, perfect day, timed writing, tourist in your own town, the Doubt envelope, the door.
