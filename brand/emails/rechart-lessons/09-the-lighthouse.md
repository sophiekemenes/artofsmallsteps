**Subject:** Lesson 9: Light your lighthouse

**Preview text:** Why reaching a goal can still feel empty, and the four writings that help you avoid it.

---

*[Listen to this lesson instead →]([AUDIO-LINK])*

Hi {{Nickname}},

Look at your map for a moment. A compass in the corner. In the middle, your cube, your house, your five pillars. You know what matters to you, and you know what a good day is made of.

That's already a lot. Plenty of people never get this far.

But it isn't enough yet. Because right now, you're still drifting.

## Drift, fight, or swim

There's a saying: only dead fish go with the flow. There's some truth in it. If you always just hand yourself over to circumstances, to whatever the world happens to be doing, you won't meet much resistance. But nothing will happen to you that's really about you, either.

There's another saying, slightly less elegant: don't spit into the wind. Also true. I'll spare you the details of how most people learn that one. And it goes for life too: if you always head straight for the biggest resistance, you put in an enormous amount of energy for a tiny result.

So if drifting is no good, and fighting the current is no good either, what's left?

The answer is right there: go with the current, and use the currents and winds that work in your favour. Swim. Or, since we're at sea: sail.

The events of life happen around you anyway. But with small decisions, you can keep correcting the course that decides where your life ends up. That's why it isn't enough to know what's important to you. You also need to know which way to go if you want to find those important things. Your decisions, one after another, steer your life. And the bigger stops along the way, the ones that really set the direction, are your goals.

## From values to goals

Your values are where your goals come from.

Say love is your number one. Then your goals might include:

- bringing out your best inner qualities
- learning to talk, and to listen, really well
- feeling at home in your own skin
- getting good at the things that matter most in a relationship
- finding a partner who truly fits you
- building a lasting, deep, joyful relationship

Notice that these are already GOALS, not values.

But before you charge off at full steam, let's stop for a moment.

It isn't always obvious what we need to do to really get where we want to be. Because a goal is also a state. Where you are now, your current situation, is your starting state. What you want, your changed circumstances, is your goal state. Setting a goal is about getting you from A to B.

But what if, after a long effort and real changes, maybe even real sacrifices, you arrive at your goal state, and for reasons nobody can explain, you're not happy?

You bought the house. You're sitting in it. And you're in a bad mood.

You got to the top of your career. You're a respected lawyer, you're swimming in money, and you're fed up with everything.

You live with your three children in a happy family, truly in love with your partner, and yet... you lie awake at night.

What could be behind this?

Most of the time, it's that your goal wasn't checked against your higher purpose.

Let me explain what that is.

## The goal of goals

Your higher purpose is the goal of your goals. The state you want to reach through the goals you've achieved. Longer term, nobler, more serious, more important.

- You wanted the house so you'd feel financially secure, but the mortgage is so big that it only makes you anxious.
- You became a lawyer so your knowledge would be recognised and people would look up to you, but somehow that just isn't part of your life.
- You started a family to live in happiness and love with your children and your partner, but deep down you're longing to travel and see the world. You miss adventure, and that ship, it seems, has sailed.

It matters a great deal that you find your higher purpose. Only then can you set the smaller goals precisely.

## Task · Higher

You already have your Pen Companion. Today it gets its biggest job so far. We're going to ask it to help us find your higher purpose.

Four writings. Handwritten, please, and keep the pen moving.

**1. Six minutes of free writing**

Write freely, without stopping, for six minutes. No set topic. It can be anything at all.

**2. A fantastic year**

Imagine that you've set goals for the next twelve months. Imagine, too, that everything goes as well as it possibly can, and a year from now you've achieved a whole lot of great things.

Now write down what you do in this new, changed life when you wake up in the morning.

Write by hand, without stopping, for at least six minutes. First person, present tense: *I wake up...*

For example:

*"The alarm doesn't go off, I wake up by myself. I go to the bathroom, make a coffee and sit down at my desk. I write for ten minutes, and great ideas come to me while I'm writing. Then I switch on my computer and read my emails. Several are from people who are clients of my new business. I'm glad I took the plunge. I don't know why I was so scared of it before, I'm already earning more than I used to. Once I've read my emails, I get dressed and go for a run. I listen to music while I run, and I feel great. I breathe in the fresh air, and the smell of fresh pastries outside the bakery on the corner, I smile at the people coming the other way, and I can feel myself filling up with energy. Back home I shower and change. I spend two hours at the computer, writing and making things. Then I head out, because I have meetings in person too... Before I step out of the door, I look in the mirror. I'm happy with who I see..."*

It doesn't matter how far you get. It's not about describing the whole day. Write for at least six minutes, or longer.

**3. More great years**

Now imagine that what you've just written was only the first stop on a wonderful journey. That was the starting point, and since then more wonderful years have gone by, and the road you've travelled has been your achievement. Everything has turned out as well as it could. You raised the bar, and you sailed over it. Great new goals have come true, and you owe it all to yourself.

Fly forward in time, settle into that moment, and once again write how a day of yours goes. At least six minutes, by hand, without stopping. First person, present tense again.

**4. At the summit**

More years have gone by, and you've come into harbour. Your highest goal is real now. You're not dreaming about it any more, you're living it.

One morning you lean back in your chair, happy and content...

Now write down what's going on inside you in this future moment. What are you thinking about? How do you feel? What have you achieved?

Write down, too, how other people see you. What do they say when they talk about you?

That's a serious piece of writing. I hope it filled you with joy and a bit of fire, {{Nickname}}.

## Name it

After the four writings, have a coffee, or go for a walk. Take a little break.

Then write in your notebook what you're naming as your higher purpose. Your life purpose.

Maybe there's just one thing you want to make happen. Maybe you'll happily list several. Please be specific! Don't write "I want to be happy." Write what will make you happy.

There's a wonderful Japanese word, *ikigai*. In the West it's usually translated as "your reason for getting up every morning". That's a simplification, to be honest: the original idea is much richer, it holds a sense of wholeness too, and the fact that all this connects you to other people, not only inward. But the part I brought it here for still stands. Many people live their whole lives without ever holding that reason in their hands. Or rather, it wanders around some corner of their heart or mind, but gets lost in the net of daily wants, and its essence slips through their fingers.

You won't make that mistake. Your higher purpose is within reach now. It's like the flag on a ship at sea, or a lantern in the dark. Steering towards it, in any area of your life, gives you encouragement and faith, even when you sometimes feel very alone, or lost.

## Your map · Layer 3: the lighthouse

Take your big sheet again.

Go to the far edge, as far from your cube and your house as the paper allows. That's your horizon. Draw a lighthouse there. A tall stripy one, a stubby one, a stick with a light on top: it really doesn't matter.

Then draw its light, the beams going out across the sheet, and write your higher purpose in them.

Now look at the whole map. Home port in the middle. Lighthouse on the horizon. And between them, a lot of empty sea.

Good. That's exactly how it should look today. Next time, we start finding what's out there.

And keep this in mind: this isn't daydreaming. This is already goal-setting. We're building something real.

Take this game seriously.

With love,
Sophie
