Hi {{Nickname}},

We've set off. Welcome on deck.

I've been thinking, and I've come to the conclusion that at the very start we can't quite get away without a bit of theory. Theory, yes, but not dry, and not even boring. I promised you this would be about you. Well, this is about you too. Just watch.

I'm convinced we live in a rather special age. The patterns of how we think have been the same for thousands of years: to use a computer example, you get a piece of hardware, your body, which you can upgrade and look after… and you get software, the self-learning kind, which runs on whatever information it picks up from the world around it.

The operating system of that software, the thing quietly running all the background processes… well, until now you haven't had much say in what it's made of. From childhood on, it was put together from the "inputs" of your experiences and the emotional reactions you had to them, and it became something completely unique. Its job is to get you through life. To protect you, to keep you safe.

Nobody in the whole wide world thinks the way you do. Nobody sees the same thing exactly the way you see it.

Alongside your own experiences, you also get a bit of an inheritance from the distant past: the ancestral programs of belonging to a group, a family; reproduction, keeping the species going. And there's a built-in emergency brake in you that still flashes a big red DANGER sign whenever it senses your life is at risk, only it hasn't noticed that the circumstances have changed. This brake works as if it still had to protect you from bears and lions, as if you were walking among hostile tribes. Civilisation is a foreign word to it. It's fast, automatic, and it doesn't really stop to ask you whether the alarm is justified at all. That's why we still overreact in so many situations that aren't actually dangerous in the slightest. A perfect example is the fear of speaking in public: the cold sweat, the shaking hands, the tight throat all belong to the fear of death. And it's fairly unlikely you'll die giving that presentation. Still, something in you can only react this way.

Here's another perfect example. Stand next to the shower and turn on the cold water. Let the chill of it hit your face, put your fingers in, feel how cold it is, a mere ten degrees.

If I now asked you to step under that icy spray with your whole body, it would be the perfect moment to watch your gut reactions.

You know the cold water isn't life-threatening.
You know nothing bad can happen to you.
You know the cold will be a passing discomfort.
That afterwards you'll step out, dry off and warm up again.
And yet a mental barrier stands in your way, the kind that quietly comes along with you through your whole life.

So taking that one step under the cold spray becomes a challenge.
And if you were paying attention, you've just met one of your hidden programs.

And oh, there are lots of them. The most popular comparison is the iceberg, where only the tip sticks out of the water and two thirds of it sprawls invisibly underneath, but that one's been picked to pieces so many times.

Let's call it something else instead:

**the downstairs neighbour.**

Your subconscious. The keeper of the keys to your real successes and changes. A very sweet neighbour, actually, but with a rather peculiar logic. Since it doesn't judge, it only experiences, that logic can be quite childlike. It very easily puts an equals sign between things that make no rational sense together, for example: speaking in public = mortal danger.

At the same time, here in the 21st century, we've reached the point where any ordinary person can take the programming of this strange, sweet neighbour-software into their own hands. We can teach it to run more useful programs instead of the old ones we know aren't good for us.

We already have the information to work out how we function, how many things we're made of. We can explore, shyly and curiously, the world of our instincts and our subconscious, where a big part of our decisions come from.

Now we can get to know, get to like, and gently tame the less loved or less useful parts of our personality.

And that view, {{Nickname}}, holds a lot of possibilities. :)

## Task 1 · Introduce yourself to your downstairs neighbour

One of the main signs of the downstairs neighbour is that it loves the language of symbols and metaphors, and it communicates brilliantly through them.

(If you haven't played Find Your Bearings yet, pause here and play it first, before you read on. Five minutes, a pen, and the key at the end: [Find your bearings →](https://artofsmallsteps.com/wayfinder-method/find-your-bearings/?from=rechart))

You've already met it, actually. Remember the cube, the ladder, the lion? That little game in Find Your Bearings was your first conversation with your downstairs neighbour.

Let me tell you about one of the first times I ever played it with someone.

In my early twenties I spent a summer in Spain, in Benidorm, a famously lively beach resort, painting henna tattoos, on a summer adventure with a boy. One evening I took him through the game. His cube was a human-sized cardboard box. And when the lion came, he jumped into it.

I won't tell you much more about him. But his ego really was fragile, and when life threw problems at him, he really did shut himself away inside himself, hoping the claws wouldn't find him in there.

Now, here's the important part. The key is only a signpost. It's not a verdict, and it's not a diagnosis. It helps us most when we let ourselves name what we saw, say it out loud, fill it with as many details as we can. And when we're willing to accept that something in us might actually think this way.

Because that's the mindset we're reaching for now: to approach, as flexibly as we can, someone we believe we know really well, and who can still always surprise us. Ourselves.

## Task 2 · Deepen the conversation

Take your notebook and write down your answers. All of them: the cube, the ladder, the lion, the road, the sea, the house and the sign, the horse. We'll be using this notebook for lots of things. Keep this page. These pictures will turn up again later, on a map.

Remember what I said about nobody seeing and understanding the same thing the way you do? Here too, the key only offers a parallel. To actually interpret it, you need to call on your own inner intuition. If you saw the sea as stormy, but you happen to love a stormy sea, then it's a positive symbol, because it's dear to you.

Go through your answers like this. Notice which one lights up for you, which one carries an extra message. During "games" like this, wishes, longings or goals that have so far only wandered around the far corners of our mind often come into focus.

And one more question to ask yourself, and to think about over the next two days:

If I, as the storyteller, gave you one more magic wand in the story above… which area, which thing would you change? You can only choose one, but it'll become exactly what you'd like it to be.

Is it the cube, the ladder, or the horse? The road, perhaps?

You decide.

We won't go into why this matters just yet, but write it down, or circle it.
