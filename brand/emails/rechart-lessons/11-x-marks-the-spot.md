Hi {{Nickname}},

It's starting to take shape, isn't it? How many different sides there are to setting a goal properly.

By now you've given your downstairs neighbour plenty of work, and let's be honest, you haven't been slacking either: you've spent a lot of conscious thought on things most people very rarely think about.

You have a whole pile of cards by now. Before the sheer number of them scares you off, let me tell you: the real point is only coming now. When, out of all those many things, you end up with a few goals that you know for sure have made it through the sieve, checked from every side. Goals that, if you make them happen, every deep inner part of you, every belief and every role, will back you up.

{{Nickname}}, always remember: this is the maths of a better life and of happiness. Never forget it :)

Today we finish the map. Four tasks, and one of them is pure fun. Let's start with that one.

## Task 1 · Role swap

Be open, and play a little. See what it feels like to slip into another role!

One of the best and most uplifting ways to do it: be a tourist in your own town for a day. Plan a day, and look at your town as if you'd travelled abroad. Go to a museum, or to places and neighbourhoods you always just rush through, or haven't seen in ages. Walk around with your eyes open: notice the carvings above doorways, the statues tucked into the fronts of buildings, the clever bits of architecture. If the weather allows, walk, and do what you'd do in Barcelona, Nepal or Timbuktu: be amazed by everything. Watch the people, and see a face of your town that you normally don't see, or let slip past you.

A new role, one you chose yourself, gives us new filters, and we come away richer. We collect experiences we can hold on to later.

If you're feeling experimental, a few more ideas:

- next time you go out with friends, be the long-lost heir of a small European royal family, travelling in disguise, for one evening
- for one day, act as if you were 17... or 82
- for one day, step into your neighbour's shoes and look at the world through their eyes. How does it feel?
- Is there someone you really look up to? Try on their shoes for a little while.

Try on as many roles as you can. And once you've settled back into yourself, you might even decide to keep a few of them. If they serve your inner goals, they can be a great help.

After any of the above, close the experience with at least five minutes of intuitive writing. Your Pen Companion knows the way.

And remember what I asked of you a while ago? While you're here, always think of yourself as the main character. Whatever the film, whatever roles you play in it, the camera should see you!

If you ever forget, just pick up your pen. :)

## Task 2 · Values meet roles

Now take out your two piles of cards: your values from lesson 7, and your roles from lesson 10.

How could you combine them?

If one of your values is family, you can put the parent role next to it, or partner, or the one who hosts Sunday lunch.

If the value is career, it might go with the shrewd businessperson, the keen researcher, or the hard-working student.

Sort your cards into little piles, a value with the roles that belong to it. Then put one more card next to each pile. Make it a coloured one if you can: you know those lovely coloured card sheets? Cut a piece from one of those. It can be bigger than the others, as big as a sheet of writing paper, or half of one.

## Task 3 · Goal cards

On these new cards we're going to write GOALS. Well, not "we". You. That's the task. Write goals on them.

For example, for the partner card from last time, you might write:

- We bring back date night, every other week, no excuses.
- I spend more real time with them. (Phone in the other room. Yes, really.)
- I plan a surprise so good their jaw drops.

You can write several similar goals on one card. Similar goals are similar because they share the same higher purpose: creating that value in your life, or living those roles at a higher level.

Take your time. We need these goals.

## Task 4 · The one

And now, the moment the whole map has been waiting for.

Look at all your goal cards, and pick one goal. The biggest, the most important one, the one that will make the biggest splash when you set off towards it. Don't go small on this. (I'll contradict myself on that soon enough, but not today.)

Open your notebook to a fresh page, and write that goal at the top. That's the page we'll be working on in the third stage.

## Your map · Layer 5: X marks the spot

Last layer. Your map is about to be finished.

Look at your islands. For each goal on your goal cards, find the island, the role, it belongs to, and mark a small x on it.

Then find the one. Your big goal. Mark it with a big, bold X, in the brightest colour you have. If you like, write the goal next to it, small.

Now step back and look at the whole sheet.

A compass in the corner. Home port in the middle, with your five pillars around it. A lighthouse on the horizon. Islands, some lush, some pale, some with firm coastlines and some dotted. And one big X.

That's your other map. Nobody else in the world has this one.

Don't draw the route yet. Between your home port and your X there's open sea, and out at sea there's weather. That's what Stage 3 is for: we'll look at what might rock the boat, what you're carrying on board, and how to plan the voyage. The route comes then.

But tonight, just enjoy it. You drew a map. A real one.

With love,
Sophie
