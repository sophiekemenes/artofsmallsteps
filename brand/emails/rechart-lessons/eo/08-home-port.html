Pinning down your values is a very easy task and a fiendishly hard one at the same time. That's why, once people have done it, they group things, analyse, think it over, and most of the time realise that narrowing it down to five is anything but easy. Well done for not putting it off. Your values are the foundation for everything we build from here.

Today I'd like to give you a gentle, simple companion to them.

Remember how much we talked about how good it is to look ahead, or look deep? To believe in the future, and to keep our values close, the way a good owner rests a hand on their dog's head?

The trouble is that in everyday life, the big abstract words, freedom, love, friendship, peace... well, they feel far away. We live minute by minute, while our mind keeps scanning the past and the future, and sometimes fills our thoughts with a lot of noise.

Do you remember being very small? I mean early childhood. When time was measured in "how many sleeps until...".

I adore that unit of time. It cuts time into neat, friendly pieces: from the moment you get up to the moment you go to bed. And how many of those pieces you need before you get to the thing you're longing for.

As grown-ups it's different, of course. We're already thinking about tomorrow's meetings and duties, we plan ahead, so "sleeps" aren't much of a unit any more. Sometimes sleep is just a compulsory break between all the things we have to do.

Still. Let's make this a little different today.

Just for fun.

Because your values are the most secret pillars of who you are, the thing you can always lean on, from the inside. Values are wonderful. But if you honestly look at an ordinary day of yours, you might not find them there at all.

What you will find is lots of other things that don't wear such a lofty label, and still matter very, very much.

Task 1 · The Perfect Day

Lean back and relax. Think back to the summers of your childhood, and let your inner child come up to the surface a little. As a child, you knew exactly what a perfect day would look like. As a child, you owned your time differently. That's who we want to meet now! :)

Of course, you might find that attitude in a different period of your life. Then use the time you most like to remember.

I'd like you to imagine how a perfect day would go. Really perfect. You wake up. What do you do next? Start with the coffee, or the scrambled eggs. Then what happens? Where do you go? Who do you meet? What do you do? Don't take off completely from your life as it is now: build the best possible day out of what's within your reach right now. Put in lunch. The afternoon. Places, things that happen. The early evening. Dinner.

Be shameless :) I mean it. Let that day be perfect, from the moment you open your eyes to the moment you close them at night.

Task 2 · Pillars

Look back over that day. Pick out five things, the five most important ones, the ones that made that day what it was: as perfect as it gets. It could be a coffee drunk in peace, a brilliant workout, a hug from your child, a phone call with someone you love, or watching a film. Find and name the five things that did the most to make your day perfect.

Task 3 · Your Energy Coat of Arms

Let's draw a little, shall we?

Remember: nobody's grading this. Least of all me.

Take your big sheet, the one with the compass rose in the corner. Right in the middle, in that empty spot I asked you to save, draw your cube, as well as you can. (We had a cube back in the very first game, in Find Your Bearings, but you were free to change it afterwards. Draw the cube you feel most yourself with.)

That's you. If you like, write next to it, small: you are here.

Then take the five things that made your perfect day perfect, and write or draw them around the cube.

And one more thing from that first game. Do you remember your house? Draw it right next to the cube. That's your home. Your home port: the place every voyage starts from and comes back to.

What you have in the middle of your map now is your Energy Coat of Arms. A recipe for ending a day on something good.

And because the big sheet won't fit in your pocket, make a small copy too: on a card, on the back of an envelope, or simply take a photo of it. Keep it somewhere you can glance at it any time.

If you keep it close, you'll be able to rescue what can be rescued even after a day you'd written off as awful. Your mood, your motivation, your will.

If those five things are in your day, it's already nearly perfect. And at six in the evening, when you feel like telling the whole world where to go... you can look at your coat of arms, and do one thing from it. Just one. It might only be a tiny gesture, and it can still turn the whole day around.

That's how a day becomes a source of strength instead of a slog.

Every day that holds a little piece of your perfect day gives you strength and staying power for the voyage, wherever you decide to head. (And we're getting closer to deciding.)
