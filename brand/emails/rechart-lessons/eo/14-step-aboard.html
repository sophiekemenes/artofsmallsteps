Just as your words affect your thoughts, so does the way you hold your body.

With anchoring, back in lesson 6, we learned that nothing means anything on its own, only together with the meaning WE attach to it. But beyond our own meanings, there are body gestures we never invented. We just "got" them, and we share most of them with the rest of humanity.

Body language, of course.

When we're nervous, we bite our nails, twist our hair, drum our fingers.

When our team scores, or we suddenly pull something off, we punch the air, or throw our arms up to the sky.

Task 1 · Arriving

a) Take out your notebook, and choose the main goal you're working with.

I'd like you to travel forward in time a little, in your mind... and arrive at the moment when that goal has already come true.

If your goal is to publish your book, choose the moment you hold it in your hands for the first time and smell a copy fresh from the printer.

If your goal is a well-paid job at a company you've dreamed of working for, choose the moment they hand you your badge.

If it's the little house in the countryside, choose the moment you first put your key in the lock, and open the door... and it's yours.

We're looking for the feeling that fills us when what we wanted is already HERE.

Let yourself see it as vividly as you can: pictures, sounds, feelings. You can use the settings you experimented with for your therapeutic selfie, whatever works best for you.

Let it soak right through you.

This is what it will be like, when it's yours.

Feels good, doesn't it?

b) Hold on to the experience with five minutes of intuitive writing, so it stays with you as clearly as possible. Write freely, let it flow, about the state of having reached the goal you longed for. Your Pen Companion knows the way.

Task 2 · One door a week

a) Go to a door. Your front door, a bedroom door, the garden gate. And walk through it the way someone who has already reached that goal would.

What I'm really asking is this: from the moment you press the handle, across the threshold, and for one more step, be that person, in your body. The one who has done it.

Walk through that door the way a first-time author would. Or someone whose business is thriving. Or someone who owns a little house in the countryside.

b) Plan it in, or just do it when it feels right, but use this at least once every week.

Your map · All aboard

Take out your map one more time.

At your home port, at the very start of the route you drew in pencil, draw a small boat. Sails up.

That's you.

Every time you reach a milestone, or simply after a good week, draw the boat a little further along the route, and rub out the old one. Some weeks it'll sail a long way. Some weeks it'll bob in the same spot. Some weeks it might have to steer round a reef you hadn't spotted. All of that is the voyage.

Do it.
Doing feels good.
