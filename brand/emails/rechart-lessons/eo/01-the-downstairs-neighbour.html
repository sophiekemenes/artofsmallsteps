I'm convinced we live in a rather special age. The patterns of how we think have been the same for thousands of years: to use a computer example, you get a piece of hardware, your body, which you can upgrade and look after… and you get software, the self-learning kind, which runs on whatever information it picks up from the world around it.

The operating system of that software, the thing quietly running all the background processes… well, until now you haven't had much say in what it's made of. From childhood on, it was put together from the "inputs" of your experiences and the emotional reactions you had to them, and it became something completely unique. Its job is to get you through life. To protect you, to keep you safe.

Nobody in the whole wide world thinks the way you do. Nobody sees the same thing exactly the way you see it.

Alongside your own experiences, you also get a bit of an inheritance from the distant past: the ancestral programs of belonging to a group, a family; reproduction, keeping the species going. And there's a built-in emergency brake in you that still flashes a big red DANGER sign whenever it senses your life is at risk, only it hasn't noticed that the circumstances have changed. This brake works as if it still had to protect you from bears and lions, as if you were walking among hostile tribes. Civilisation is a foreign word to it. It's fast, automatic, and it doesn't really stop to ask you whether the alarm is justified at all. That's why we still overreact in so many situations that aren't actually dangerous in the slightest. A perfect example is the fear of speaking in public: the cold sweat, the shaking hands, the tight throat all belong to the fear of death. And it's fairly unlikely you'll die giving that presentation. Still, something in you can only react this way.

Here's another perfect example. Stand next to the shower and turn on the cold water. Let the chill of it hit your face, put your fingers in, feel how cold it is, a mere ten degrees.

If I now asked you to step under that icy spray with your whole body, it would be the perfect moment to watch your gut reactions.

You know the cold water isn't life-threatening.
You know nothing bad can happen to you.
You know the cold will be a passing discomfort.
That afterwards you'll step out, dry off and warm up again.
And yet a mental barrier stands in your way, the kind that quietly comes along with you through your whole life.

So taking that one step under the cold spray becomes a challenge.
And if you were paying attention, you've just met one of your hidden programs.

the downstairs neighbour.

Your subconscious. The keeper of the keys to your real successes and changes. A very sweet neighbour, actually, but with a rather peculiar logic.
