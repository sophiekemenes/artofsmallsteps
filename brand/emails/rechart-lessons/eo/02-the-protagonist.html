There's a term for this from the world of NLP, a slightly clunky one: submodalities. It's not some set-in-concrete scientific law, more a handy label for something I think you already know yourself: the same thing, a different interpretation, and suddenly it stirs a completely different feeling in you.

Submodalities are the qualities you yourself perceive things with. The way you translate the things of reality for yourself. Colours, shapes, time. Take seeing, the primary modality. An inner picture of yours can be close or far, big or small, bright or dim, black and white or in colour, sharp or blurred, in front of you or off to the side, and we could go on.

The qualities you give to a thing are your own unique code, your own unique relationship with that thing.

Very, very simply put: if to you white stands for cleanliness, innocence and hope, THEN if your cube in the desert game was white, it's already carrying a very important message for you. Namely, what you really think of yourself.

Submodalities make a great playground for the playful kind of self-development, and we'll spend a lot more time with them later. But let's get back to you, because we haven't forgotten that everything here, if you please, is about you, have we? :)

Task 1 · The therapeutic selfie

By now it's fair to say that everyone, whatever their age, has taken a selfie. This time I'd like you to sit down, using your phone's built-in filters or your favourite app, and analyse a bit more consciously what you actually think of the different filters.

Notice which colours and what sharpness mean "beautiful" to you. Which filter makes you most the "lead"?

The bluish tones, or the yellowish ones?

Are you mad about vibrant, vivid colours, do you feel your most gorgeous, your most attractive in pictures like that?

Or does black and white win the day?

As much as your time allows, consciously work out your reactions to each colour and sharpness, and note them down in your notebook!

Once you've done it, there you go: another key in your hand, to open a treasure chest inside you once we get that far.

Task 2 · Cube-ing

Please take the cube you imagined in the game… and simply start changing it, as an experiment. Give it a different colour. Make it bigger, or smaller. Its material softer, or harder. If it didn't have a function before, give it one, turn it into something you could use.

After every change, turn it back into the original, and compare.

What do you feel? What did the change add, what did it take away? Are you filled with uncomfortable or pleasant feelings while you make that change?

Play with this for as long as you like.

Task 3 · Write it down!

Maybe something in you has already stirred: it would be a shame for all this information to vanish into thin air. Use your notebook, and write down everything you experience during the exercises! In this first stage we mostly use our imagination, but because your mind is flexible, sometimes this information slips away by the next day, to make room for "more useful" things, like a PIN number.
