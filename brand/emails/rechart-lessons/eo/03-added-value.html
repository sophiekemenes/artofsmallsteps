Alice was alone in the forest. She was cold and on edge. The reason she'd left her safe little nook, to find the bird who loves in three languages, already felt like a distant picture. The whole journey seemed pointless. Her courage and her stamina were starting to flag, and the lights of home, despite her eight teasing sisters, despite her mother's cantankerous ways, now seemed far friendlier. She trudged along sulkily, watching the sunlight filter through the trees. Twigs cracked under her feet, the leaves rustled, until she stepped on something soft.

"Ow! Hey!" came a sudden voice.

Alice gave a little shriek and jumped back.

"And now she's the one getting all huffy and screeching…" grumbled a voice, and a tiny creature emerged, brushing off the leaves. "As if she wasn't the one tramping on people's feet like a walking sideboard!"

Alice blushed. "I'm sorry… um…"

"Mokka. My name is Mokka. And yes, thank you, I was sleeping beautifully until you walked over me."

The corners of Alice's mouth turned down. That was all she needed.

"I do beg your pardon…" she said, moments away from bursting into tears out of sheer despair.

"Now… now… Erm. It's all fine," said the goblin, glancing up a little more gently into the eyes that were filling faster and faster with tears. "Don't you go turning on the waterworks here, or I'll get brain stones, I really will!"

"What are those?" Alice blurted out, just to say something, trying very hard to stay strong and really, really not cry.

"Eh. How should I know. Something you get when somebody gets on your nerves."

Alice imagined that if someone got annoyed a lot, stones would end up rattling around in their head, and she smiled faintly. You have to admit, that was quite funny.

"So what are you after round here? Because I don't suppose you're going for the world record in kicking goblins out of their sleep," the goblin asked, softening a little.

"Well, I… I'm looking for the bird who loves in three languages. I know where it lives… it's just…" the girl hung her head. "I think I've got a bit tired."

"Is that so? And have you got a sword that cuts through anything, or a talking mirror, or one of those whatsits every hero has?"

Alice looked up and frowned. She had an opened packet of chewing gum, a dried-up bread roll, her favourite notebook she liked to write in, and maybe half a pair of earrings in one of her pockets.

"No," she replied, when she'd got to the end of the inventory. "But what would I need those for?"

"Good grief, you're not a very experienced fairy-tale character, are you…" grumbled the goblin. "Well, that's what they're like these days. Off they go to hunt down legendary creatures, but a magic marble, a whistling peach? Noooo… Pfft."

"Well, I'm sorry," said the girl, a little annoyed. "I didn't know I could only go and do what I want once I'd met certain conditions!"

"Well, it can't stay like this. And that's that." The tiny hands began rummaging through the even tinier pockets of the goblin's clothes, with movements almost too quick for the eye to follow. "Here!"

Alice looked curiously at the slightly worn ballpoint pen pressed into her hand.

"The end of this has been chewed," she observed.

"So what, so what! I give her a pen and she complains! Who's ever heard of such a thing."

"I mean, thank you, I just don't understand what it's for."

"This, sweetheart, is a gift that will help you reach your goal. Everything checks out, doesn't it? An unexpected meeting with an exotic creature…" here he puffed himself up a little, "and a mysterious object that will go on to play a priceless part in the story. That's all."

"That's all?" asked Alice.

"That's all," the goblin shot back confidently.
