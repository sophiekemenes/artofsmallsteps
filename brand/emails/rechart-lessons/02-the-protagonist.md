Well, hello!

{{Nickname}}, how did you get on with the lion game the other day? I'm mostly curious about your cube, you know. Sometimes it's a really good lesson, and a really good experience, to meet something as complex as your own ego in the shape of a fairly simple object.

So, what was the cube like?

I've had hundreds of different answers to this, and not one of them was the same as another. And while we're remembering, I'd like to encourage you to think about it a bit more, and look for the hidden meanings of your cube.

Was it big, but light? Small and rock hard? Did it have a "point", a use? Was it a box, maybe, or an ice cube, or even a Rubik's cube? And if so, what else could it be used for (if it didn't happen to be sitting in the middle of a desert)?

In what kind of surroundings would this cube really come into its own?

Test it, play with the thought, and if some answer comes up, don't let it run away, write it down! Because every metaphor we question like this holds incredibly useful information we'll be able to use later, when we get to setting goals.

The flaws and unevenness of your cube might be telling you something about your doubts, and its function might even say something about how you value yourself. But that's not for me to tell you. You'll find out, if you ask.

**A reminder for later:**

Your conscious and your unconscious mind are a fantastic treasure mine, and while we're together, I'd like you to look at them that way. The treasures in there are just waiting to be found.

Some you'll have to dig for, and work a bit to get to. But some will pop straight into your hand as soon as you shine even a little light on them, and throw their arms around your neck, squealing with joy that you've finally found them. :)

There's a term for this from the world of NLP, a slightly clunky one: **submodalities.** It's not some set-in-concrete scientific law, more a handy label for something I think you already know yourself: the same thing, a different interpretation, and suddenly it stirs a completely different feeling in you.

Submodalities are the qualities you yourself perceive things with. The way you translate the things of reality for yourself. Colours, shapes, time. Take seeing, the primary modality. An inner picture of yours can be close or far, big or small, bright or dim, black and white or in colour, sharp or blurred, in front of you or off to the side, and we could go on.

The qualities you give to a thing are your own unique code, your own unique relationship with that thing.

Very, very simply put: if to you white stands for cleanliness, innocence and hope, THEN if your cube in the desert game was white, it's already carrying a very important message for you. Namely, what you really think of yourself.

Submodalities make a great playground for the playful kind of self-development, and we'll spend a lot more time with them later. But let's get back to you, because we haven't forgotten that everything here, if you please, is about you, have we? :)

Listen, {{Nickname}}!

For this little while we're spending together, I'd like you to think of yourself as the protagonist. The lead.

Everyone knows the world of Hollywood films. It's no secret how many filters they use to make sure the leads always shine in their best light.

(And no, we're not talking now about planting false beauty ideals in humanity's mind. Here we measure beauty and shine by your own inner standard.)

Let me show you a very simple example of how submodalities, filters, can change the feelings in you about the SAME thing.

Here I am.

Hi! :D

This is a photo, and let's take it as reality. If I'm very strict, I can find my flaws, but basically it gives off (to me) a pleasant sort of harmony.

Let's turn the filter down a bit.

[IMAGE 2: dark filter]

Ooh, rough, isn't it? You might not get into an elevator with me. Involuntary associations attach themselves to the picture: it suggests danger, and bad intentions.

Let's turn it up a bit.

[IMAGE 3: fairytale filter]

Oh my, now this is like a fairy tale! Isn't that a princess fluttering her lashes, waiting for her tousle-haired prince, the wonderful burdens of a honey-sweet world in her eyes?

You see?
The thing is the same. The picture is the same. Nothing has changed.

Only the filter. In other words, the submodalities.

And yet the whole world can turn upside down, and everything takes on the opposite meaning.

It's that simple… and that wonderful.

That's how your mind works too. What's more, your brain was using far more filters long before computers or smartphones ever existed.

That's how reality gets reshaped into feelings, thoughts, opinions.

That's how a frog becomes a prince, and a pumpkin a carriage. Or the other way round, at midnight.

Most of the time, even in the mirror, you don't see reality, you see whichever filter you happen to be using.

Fun, isn't it?

## Task 1 · The therapeutic selfie

By now it's fair to say that everyone, whatever their age, has taken a selfie. This time I'd like you to sit down, using your phone's built-in filters or your favourite app, and analyse a bit more consciously what you actually think of the different filters.

Notice which colours and what sharpness mean "beautiful" to you. Which filter makes you most the "lead"?

The bluish tones, or the yellowish ones?

Are you mad about vibrant, vivid colours, do you feel your most gorgeous, your most attractive in pictures like that?

Or does black and white win the day?

As much as your time allows, consciously work out your reactions to each colour and sharpness, and note them down in your notebook!

Once you've done it, there you go: another key in your hand, to open a treasure chest inside you once we get that far.

## Task 2 · Cube-ing

Please take the cube you imagined in the game… and simply start changing it, as an experiment. Give it a different colour. Make it bigger, or smaller. Its material softer, or harder. If it didn't have a function before, give it one, turn it into something you could use.

After every change, turn it back into the original, and compare.

What do you feel? What did the change add, what did it take away? Are you filled with uncomfortable or pleasant feelings while you make that change?

Play with this for as long as you like.

## Task 3 · Write it down!

Maybe something in you has already stirred: it would be a shame for all this information to vanish into thin air. Use your notebook, and write down everything you experience during the exercises! In this first stage we mostly use our imagination, but because your mind is flexible, sometimes this information slips away by the next day, to make room for "more useful" things, like a PIN number. But if you read it back over the coming weeks, it'll give you ideas and new points of view. So off you go! :)
