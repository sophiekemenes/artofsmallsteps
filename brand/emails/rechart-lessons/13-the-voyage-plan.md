Hi {{Nickname}},

Last time we sat down with the dragons. You gave them names, a seat in the back, and maybe even a little collar.

So now it's time for the part a lot of people want to start with. The part where we actually plan the voyage.

I'll be honest: I'm glad we didn't start here. Without knowing your values, your home port and your lighthouse, a plan is just a list of things to do. Now it's a course. Everything you've done so far was the run-up. This is where you jump.

Today we're going to look at your big goal from a lot of different sides, and pack everything you'll need for the journey. It's a long letter, so get comfortable. Make a cup of something.

## Your goal, on a fresh page

Open your notebook to the page where you wrote your big goal in lesson 11.

Let me show you how this works with an example. Say the goal is:

*I want a little family house in the countryside.*

And remember, every goal has a higher purpose behind it, a much bigger one. In this case, maybe something like: *a beautiful little house in a lovely spot, where I live happily with my loving partner, who is also my soulmate and best friend, and our children, and where we build our own business, helping people find their own happiness.*

Keep that in mind. Now, six lists.

## List 1 · Reasons: the wind in your sails

Why do you want to reach this goal?

**REASONS:**

- I want to get away from the noise of the city.
- I want calm and peace.
- I'd like to welcome my future clients somewhere beautiful.
- It's what I've always wanted.

And so on.

The reasons are the wind in your sails. The more you put in, the further they'll carry you.

Take as long as you like over these. That goes for every list in this letter.

## List 2 · Obstacles: the reefs

What obstacles do I have to get past to reach my goal?

**OBSTACLES:**

- I haven't got enough money.
- With my current job, it's very hard to make a move.
- I'm scared it won't work out. I can't quite believe in it.
- I don't really know anything about buying a house.
- I'm no handyperson, and I'm afraid the renovation will hold a lot of nasty surprises.
- I have a loan that I can barely keep up with, and that could be a problem.

We're not looking for solutions yet. We're only charting the reefs. Once you know where they are, you can plan a route that sails elegantly around them, instead of crashing straight into the rocks.

(You might recognise a few of these from your Doubt envelope. That's fine. A dragon and a reef can live in the same spot.)

## List 3 · Inner resources you already have: the fuel

What inner resources do you have that support this goal?

An inner resource is something you can use to reach your goals, but it isn't a physical thing. It's a quality, an ability, an attitude. Something that's yours, and that you couldn't put in a wheelbarrow.

**INNER RESOURCES I HAVE:**

- I'm really enthusiastic.
- I love people.
- I don't give up easily.
- I speak a second language well.
- I'm creative.

Your inner resources are the fuel for the journey.

## List 4 · Inner resources still missing

Is there anything missing that you'll need? Because that's the next list.

**INNER RESOURCES I STILL NEED:**

- Courage to change
- The ability to land on my feet, whatever happens
- Communication skills
- Some know-how, or experience, in buying property

These are all the things you don't have yet, but would need. And notice: every single one of them can be learned.

## List 5 · Outer resources you already have: the supplies

Let's not forget the outer resources. These support your goal too, but they don't come from inside you. They come from outside. Some of them you could put in a wheelbarrow (a laptop). Some you couldn't (an influential friend, unless they drink themselves under the table).

Let's start with what you've got:

**OUTER RESOURCES I HAVE:**

- a laptop and a phone with internet
- a car
- lots of good friends
- my current job
- a whole lot of books

## List 6 · Outer resources still missing

And what do you still need?

**OUTER RESOURCES I STILL NEED:**

- a friend who knows the property market
- money
- money
- money

Outer resources make the journey easier. Sometimes you can't get very far without them. But here's the important bit: most of the time, it's your inner resources that get you the outer ones.

## Let's stop here for a minute

You could work on these lists for days, and you're very welcome to. Right now, we're only looking at your goal from lots of new angles. The more sides you see it from, the more accurate your picture of it gets. (Remember the map and the territory?)

I want to give you two things with all this. One is the points of view. The other is the ability, and the willingness, to pick up any item on any list and work with it. Any time.

I'm sure you already know far more about your goal than you did an hour ago. And you could quite happily spend a whole week on just one thing.

In the example, say, money. You ask yourself questions, and look at money itself from every side. How could I earn more? How could I spend less? How could I buy a house at the lowest possible price? Get rid of the loan? Replace money with something else? And so on.

So you have a view from many sides now, and the chance to set off along any of them and find new, creative solutions.

## Ten things that will get you there

Now I'll give you TEN more points of view. Rules, tasks, tips, call them what you like. Hold your goal up against each one, reshape it where it needs it, and by the end you'll know exactly what you have to do. They come from a mix of different goal-setting approaches, and I've kept each one short.

**1 · Say it in the positive**
With values, there are ones you move towards and ones you move away from. With goals, only the first kind. Your goal can't be to get rid of an illness. It can only be to be healthy. It's so logical it's almost obvious, but I'm writing it down anyway, so you don't trip over it.

**2 · Take it apart**
Remember how I told you not to go small on your big goal, and said I'd contradict myself soon? Here it is. You can reach even the biggest goal if you break it into small enough pieces. Your goal is part of something bigger, your higher purpose. And your goal has parts too: the bigger ones are your MILESTONES, their parts are tasks, and the smallest pieces, the ones you don't break down any further, are habits. Habits will take you all the way to the biggest goal. Slowly, but surely.

**3 · Have a plan**
Always have a plan. You know how we work by now: we think, we mull it over, we weigh it up, and then we choose the best option. Know exactly where you're going from and where you're going to, in the short term too.

**4 · Give it dates**
Unless you truly don't mind when you get there, time is one of the most important things on your journey. Make a proper timeline. Your milestones are mileposts too. Your calendar is about to become your best friend. Get ready.

**5 · Stay flexible**
You can never plan *in* the future, only *for* it. Today you decide something and make a timeline, but nobody can see ahead, so in a few months you'll need to rethink some of it. The best thing about deadlines is that you can move them. The world keeps changing, and you have to keep adjusting your course to match. Otherwise you won't end up where you set out to go. Every sailor knows this.

**6 · Put it on paper**
Write! Write down as much as you can. Once something is on paper, it's fixed, and instead of forgetting it you can keep working on it. Your written plans are the backbone of your map. Reaching a serious goal without a written plan is like sewing a suit without a pattern.

**7 · You are the centre of the world**
Only you can decide what you want. You make the plan, you make the decisions, and you're the one who acts. There can't be a single point in your plan that depends on someone else's decision. You'll need to be as stubborn as a mule. Trust me, it's a good quality.

**8 · You are not the centre of the world**
You have to think of other people too. You can't trample over everything, or drag other people's values through the mud. Take a big step back and look at your goal from there. Does it hurt anyone? Is it good for everyone involved? The aim is to find the right balance: you mustn't harm others, but others don't get a say in it. Not easy. But important.

**9 · Make it measurable**
You need to know where you are. Always. "I want to be rich" isn't measurable. "I earn 4,000 a month" (in whatever currency you dream in) is much better. Some goals are hard to measure, but luckily you can always count on your creativity. Be as specific as you possibly can.

**10 · Make it reachable**
Dare to dream big, but not too big. The impossible is nowhere near as impossible as it sounds, but if you set the bar too high, you'll just keep jumping about underneath it, and that gets very disheartening. The ideal size is a goal you'll find hard to reach, but that you absolutely can.

**11 + 12 + 13 + 14... Do it!**
The easiest way to get something done is to simply do it.

You can forget every tip above if you don't keep this last one. Plans on their own are worth nothing. A boat that never leaves the harbour doesn't go anywhere, however good its charts are. Do something EVERY DAY, and change will come into your life.

## Your map · The route

Take out your map. Today, finally, you get to draw the route.

- **Supplies.** In your home port, draw a few small barrels, crates or sacks, one for each outer resource you already have. Write a word on each if you like. Those are your supplies. You're not setting off empty-handed.
- **Reefs.** Between your home port and your X, draw your obstacles as rocks and reefs: a few jagged lines, a bit of white foam around them. You know where they are now. That's the whole point.
- **The route.** Now draw your course from home port to the X. Let it curve around the reefs. And draw it **in pencil**. Rule 5. You'll be rubbing bits of it out and redrawing them, and that's not failure. That's sailing.
- **Milestones.** Along the route, mark your milestones as little buoys, or tiny islands where you'll stop to rest and restock. Put a date next to each one.

Your dragons are still in the margins, by the way. Let them watch. They're allowed to.

## Your final task

1. Make a year plan for your goal.
2. Make a plan for next month, broken down into weeks.
3. Take action, regularly.

When the month is over:

1. Look at what you achieved this month.
2. Go through your year plan, and change it if you need to.
3. Make a plan for the next month, broken down into weeks.
4. Take action, regularly.

Repeat those last four steps every month.

Right. I won't keep you any longer, {{Nickname}}. You've got a lot to do. Stop reading me. Go and scribble!

With love,
Sophie
