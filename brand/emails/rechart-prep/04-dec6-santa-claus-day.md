Hi {{Nickname}},

Today is 6 December, and in Hungary that means Mikulás has been. He's our Santa, only earlier and a bit stricter.

Last night children all over the country polished their boots until they shone and put them in the window. By morning there's chocolate in them, tangerines, a few walnuts. If you've been good. If you haven't, you get a *virgács*: a little bundle of twigs, sometimes sprayed gold, just so you know someone's been keeping score.

(The funny part is that most bags come with both. A bit of chocolate, a bit of twig. Which is, honestly, the fairest review most of us will ever get.)

You have your own version: the naughty or nice list. Different costume, same idea. Someone, somewhere, decides what kind of child you are.

And the thing is, that verdict doesn't stay in the boot. "The clever one." "The difficult one." "Too much." "Never any trouble." We hear it early and we hear it often, and after a while it stops sounding like somebody's opinion and starts sounding like a fact. Like the weather.

Then one day, years later, something stops fitting. And most of us reach for the big eraser: new job, new city, new haircut, new me. A pottery class, just in case.

I wrote about this recently, about what it really means to rechart a life. The short version: the research on how people actually change is much kinder than the "find your new self" advice. You don't have to become someone else. You start reading your own story differently. The old failure isn't just a failure. The ten years in the wrong job weren't wasted. They're ingredients.

Same chocolate, same twigs. A different way of reading what's in the boot.

That's what we'll be doing from 3 January, one small stage at a time. Nobody keeping score.

Until then, a tiny bit of homework, if you fancy it (and only if): is there a label someone gave you a long time ago that you're still carrying around? You don't need to do anything with it. Just notice it. Write it on a scrap of paper if you like.
