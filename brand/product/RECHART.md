# RECHART — the English-language program

**Working name for the global, English-language version of Újratervezés.** This is the product-structure decision this session was missing; it replaces the flat 15-lesson placeholder list in `/site/src/pages/program/`.

Source: Sophie's own note ("RECHART — logikai megfeleltetés az ÚT eredeti anyagával"), uploaded 2026-09-28. Reproduced here as-is (Hungarian original kept verbatim below the summary) because it is itself the working spec, not raw source material to be adapted later.

## The core idea

Not a "fix yourself" metaphor — a navigation metaphor. Life doesn't always need a new destination; sometimes it needs you to stop, get your bearings, recalibrate your inner navigator, and look at the map differently.

**Core brand idea:** *You don't need someone to tell you where to go. You need to know how to navigate.*

**Tagline:** *RECHART — Finding your way through storms.*

## Relationship to the Art of Small Steps host brand

Not a tension with the "quiet way to change" thesis — the same root, two angles:

- **Art of Small Steps** = mercy, gentleness — how you treat *yourself* during the process
- **RECHART** = the concrete navigation tool — held gently, not as a weapon

Where "storm" appears in RECHART copy, it is never a dramatic enemy to defeat — it's simply what's there. The response to it is not struggle-rhetoric but patient, kind sailing. This is *why* RECHART doesn't romanticize struggle: because the answer to the storm is mercy, not combat.

## The three phases

### 1. RECALIBRATE YOUR INNER NAVIGATOR *(= warm-up / preparation)*
Where you actually stand right now — not the destination, an exact read of the present moment.

Maps to original lessons 1–6: the Downstairs Neighbor (instinctive reactions / inner programs — the navigator's "engine room"), the Protagonist (cube/ladder/road/shore metaphor system as self-examination tool), Added Value (joy-detector), Three Magic Words (linguistic recalibration: "must" → "choose"), Wish List / intuitive writing (surfacing desires, first resource-awareness).

**Untouchable elements (DO NOT TOUCH — keep exactly as in the original):** the cube, the ladder, the road, the shore, the lion, the Downstairs Neighbor as metaphor, the pen-as-wand, the joy-detector.

**Scientific anchor:** self-monitoring / attentional processes; self-distancing (the metaphor objects — the cube, the Downstairs Neighbor — create psychological distance before you name anything about yourself).

### 2. BUILD YOUR OTHER MAP *(= planning)*
Once the navigator is calibrated, you get to consciously name the direction you were already building toward. Not that the old map was wrong — that this one is now deliberately yours.

Maps to original lessons 7–12: Value Bubbles (330/165 value list, bubble method), Energy Coat-of-Arms (perfect day, pillars), One (or more) Above All (core-value hierarchy, goal-value fit), What Hides in the Dark (fears, ikigai, dialogue with inner parts), Roles and Qualities (roles → values → abilities → goals bridge — the product's core IP), Does Good Wine Need a Label? (goal-sifting, alignment to a higher purpose).

**Scientific anchor:** self-concordance / Self-Determination Theory (autonomous motivation), identity-based approaches (roles), cognitive reappraisal (reframing fears/inner parts).

### 3. STORMPROOF YOUR VESSEL *(= action)*
Turns insight into motion. Obstacles, resources, practical constraints, support systems, next steps, changing conditions — together, in one phase. Not about enthusiasm — about staying in motion when the weather changes.

Maps to original lessons 13–15: inner resources (start of lesson 13), posture/embodying the achieved goal (lesson 14 — the practice stays, drop the "scientific" power-pose justification), obstacles/external resources/concrete plan/milestones/habits (end of lesson 13), closing — "you are not your behavior" (lesson 15, Parting Words).

**Scientific anchor:** mental contrasting + implementation intentions (MCII/WOOP) — the biggest scientific anchor found in the original audit; fits exactly here: desired future + real obstacle + concrete "if this happens, then I do this" plan.

**Open question, not yet built:** "storm" currently plays only on the storm itself. Sophie flagged that reality also includes *stillness* (motivational loss, stalling, no wind) — not built into this version, but worth noting as a future extension (a "when there's no wind" sub-section) if the product expands. Fits the brand-core's "changing conditions" phrasing already.

## Brand world & tone

Vocabulary: maps, navigation, bearings, wayfinding, vessels, weather, currents, horizons, signals, routes, rerouting, charts — as an intelligent tool, never a sailor costume.

Tone: intelligent, warm, curious, lightly cheeky; playful but not childish; thought-provoking but not solemn; metaphorical but not mystical; psychologically grounded but not clinical; practical but not productivity-coaching.

**RECHART does NOT:**
- tell anyone what their life should look like
- promise a completely transformed "new life"
- offer a universal life recipe
- treat uncertainty as failure
- claim there's one correct route
- romanticize struggle or "the storm"
- use generic motivational language

**RECHART DOES:**
- help you notice where you actually stand
- help you tell your own signals apart from outside noise
- help surface what matters
- help imagine alternative routes
- work with what you already have
- turn reflection into possible action
- treat rerouting as a legitimate part of navigating

**Core philosophical line:** *You don't need the whole map before you start. You need enough awareness to choose the next step.*

## Consistency check against the Hungarian source audit

Confirmed aligned, not just in tone but in scientific/ethical rigor, with what `source-material/ujratervezes-hu/ANALYSIS.md` already flagged:
- The audit warned against "best year of your life / total transformation" over-promising → RECHART's "does not promise a completely transformed 'new life'" rules this out directly.
- The audit noted the product's real strength isn't a single "correct answer" but self-authored meaning (e.g. the cube metaphor) → RECHART's "does not pretend there is one correct route" says the same thing in English.
- The audit cautioned against overvaluing visualization/positive-future-imagining on its own → RECHART's "reflection into **possible** action" (not guaranteed action) matches.
