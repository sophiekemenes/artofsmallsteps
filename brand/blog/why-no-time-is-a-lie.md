# Why you're lying when you say you don't have time for something (and why that's completely fine)

- **Status:** APPROVED by Sophie 2026-11-17 (translation)

When you ask someone why they aren't doing the thing they'd like to do, you usually get one of two main reasons in reply to your nosiness.

I haven't got the money.

I haven't got the time.

Both are rock solid, and they sit in front of us like heavy statements as we ride the rattling train of everyday life, day after day whooshing past, and we still haven't put our running shoes on, haven't called Grandma, haven't sat down to the lessons of the Spanish course we paid for, or the toilet is still running and I still haven't called the plumber, or there's that great idea I could turn into a blog post, and yet I don't. There's time, a certain amount of it in a day, and our duties eat all of it. I'm helpless. It's not my responsibility. It's not my fault.

Let's leave money aside and deal with time instead. Partly because it matters far more – you can always get more money, but you can't turn back time. So why is "I haven't got time" a lie? Well… actually, it isn't a lie. It's more of a brilliant sentence that you don't have to imagine anything else behind, you can just throw your hands up. Haven't got it, haven't got it. Those pesky circumstances.

Instead, I'd like to invite you to a little mind game. I promise it won't hurt, and if not an "aha", I promise you at least a "hmm" moment. NLP is very fond of getting results by changing your inner dialogue.

Listen.

When you say: I haven't got time for it, you're not saying that you haven't got time for it.

What you're actually saying is:

this isn't of primary importance to me right now. It's not a priority.

Well?

Because there are loads of other things I do, and this one I don't. I put other things further up the list and this one further down, and whoops, we've already run out of the time available. If there's something you use the no-time myth for most often, just do this much: think about what the higher purpose, the value of that activity is for you.

If it's exercise, say, the higher purpose might be health, fitness, or even that the guys/the girls/you yourself find you a bit more attractive.

If it's Spanish, then getting on at work, living abroad one day, or passing the exam.

If it's some kind of writing or making, the higher purpose might be fulfilling yourself, passing on what you've learned, being known, or living out the passion of your life.

So please, do just this: whatever you declare you haven't got time for, put the higher purpose it stands for into this sentence:

"For me, (right now) ____(higher purpose)____ is not of primary importance."

For example.

"I haven't got time to exercise." = "Right now, health and fitness are not of primary importance in my life."

That's a simple change of camera angle, and if you take in the weight of that statement, it can change a lot all by itself. Oh yes. In half a second you're no longer the victim of your circumstances, you're looking as the one in charge at something you long for and still don't do.

The first time I realised what I was actually saying, gigantic pennies came raining down from the sky. Excuse me? Hang on a minute. What do you mean, health isn't a priority? Of course it is! If you've got your health, you've got everything!

Taste it, and take in what's hiding behind your own "no time" excuse myth! Give yourself time for this statement to sink in, so that every part of you gets a little while to taste it. And then tell me: is this true for you right now?

There are surely parts of you that agree, and some that protest – it's important to reach an inner agreement with yourself, and decide. True or not?

If it's true, then let the shame or the bad feeling about the secret or obvious excuse-hunting of the "no time" myth evaporate. Until now you were the victim of your to-do list and of time, but this is where taking responsibility begins: simply by recognising and becoming aware of what matters to you. Sorry, current higher value, you didn't make the top five this time, I'm putting my creative energy somewhere else.

But if this sentence isn't true, if that thing and what it stands for really is needed and important for you to exist, to function, to fulfil yourself, your dream, to improve the quality of your life and so be happier, if you please… then lift this sentence up to you, and turn it round:

"Right now, health is of primary importance in my life, and I'm going to do something about it. And that's that."

With this simple moment you've taken the energy you used to spend in your head on the "no time" claim, and weighed it by its weight and its value. You've changed the information running along the neural pathways that kept that activity locked up, and maybe change is getting ready in you right now. And the sentence keeps growing: "Health and fitness really are a priority for me: I'll do a stretch and twenty squats right now, and tomorrow morning I'll take the stairs up to the office."

Make friends with this sentence! You've just started making time for something important. Instead of your day being made of reactions to things other people and the world roll in front of you, ending with collapsing into bed, only to carry on the same the next day, simply by the focus of your attention you've now lifted out something you really do want to spend time on! Opportunities will be served up to you on a plate once you think through how many occasions you could offer up to this one important thing.
